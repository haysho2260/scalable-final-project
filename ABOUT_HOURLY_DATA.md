# Why Hourly Predictions Matter

Even when residents are billed at flat or time-of-use (TOU) rates, hourly energy predictions provide critical operational, behavioral, and environmental value.

## 1. Physics vs. Accounting
While billing is the "accounting layer," hourly predictions capture the "physics of the system." Grid stress, renewable availability, and carbon emissions change every hour, regardless of when the bill is sent.

## 2. Optimizing Within Peak Windows
Most Time-of-Use (TOU) windows are broad (e.g., 4 PM – 9 PM). However, within that window:
*   **4–5 PM**: Demand is often just starting to ramp.
*   **6–7 PM**: Usually the moment of highest grid stress and emissions.
*   **8–9 PM**: Solar is gone, but the load is often starting to taper.

Hourly signals allow for **pre-cooling** or **delaying appliances** to the most efficient hour within a coarse billing bucket.

## 3. Powering Smart Automation
Smart devices require high-frequency signals to operate effectively.
*   **Thermostats & Heat Pumps**: Can "drift" temperatures slightly to avoid the most expensive/dirty hours.
*   **EV Chargers & Batteries**: Can align charging with exact moments of excess solar or low grid congestion.

## 4. Carbon & Environmental Impact
The carbon intensity of the grid varies significantly by the hour. Hourly predictions enable "Carbon Shifting"—moving usage to hours when the sun is shining or the wind is blowing, even if the cost to the consumer is the same.

## 5. Grid Reliability
Utilities use hourly forecasts to prevent neighborhood-level outages and manage infrastructure like transformers. Predicting residential load at this granularity helps build a more resilient and equitable energy grid for everyone.
